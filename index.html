The LIRRA Blueprint: Rewiring the Language Circuit

They often say that if you don't learn a second language as a child, you’ll never truly "own" it. I consistently observed adult learners struggle, trapped in a cycle of translating word-for-word and transferring their mother tongue constructions, never quite reaching that target-like effortless flow. My obsession became simple:

How do we "hotwire" the adult brain to acquire language as naturally as a child does?

This curiosity led to an inter-disciplinary investigation -encompassing neurocognition, psycholinguistics, corpus linguistics, and language acquisition- giving rise to the birth of the LIRRA Model—a neuroeducational revolution designed specifically for the "late learner". Instead of traditional, dry grammar drills, I dug into the architecture of the mind. I identified five key brain-adaptable features rooted in neuroscience to create a framework that speaks the brain’s native language. LIRRA isn't just a model; it’s a bridge to procedural memory—the place where habits are formed and where language finally becomes automatic.

I transformed the model into a high-energy, computerized program -The LIRRA-NP- featuring interactive gamified material, and implicit exposure designed to stimulate the brain’s reward systems.

The result?

A complete transformation in how students performed. I witnessed a more automatic performance, significant "speed-up" in production and massive gains in reading proficiency.

My students weren't just "studying" English anymore—they were processing it with enjoyment. LIRRA proved that the adult brain isn't "too old" to learn; it just needs a different set of keys to unlock its potential.

Beyond the Literal: Building a World for Every Mind

I have always been fascinated by what happens between the lines of a conversation. While my early career was spent decoding the foundations of language. My recent journey has taken me into its mechanics, diving into Neurodiversity.

For many autistic individuals, Navigating a conversation becomes a minefield of pragmatic hurdles, where the literal meaning of a word is clear, but the overarching social landscape—filled with the complex "mind-reading" required to decode language—feels like a puzzle with missing pieces. My research collaborations are dedicated to mapping this landscape and building bridges of understanding.

Walking the Spectrum: A VR Journey into Alterity

In a powerful partnership with East Stroudsburg University of Pennsylvania, I am working on a project called "From Empathy to Alterity". We are leveraging Virtual Reality (VR) to give non-autistic people a glimpse into the daily cognitive and inferential challenges faced by those on the spectrum. It’s about more than just data; it’s about fostering true inclusion by allowing people to "walk a mile" in a different cognitive world.

Where the Eyes Linger: Uncovering the Hidden Hurdles

Meanwhile, through collaborations with The University of Bristol, we are using eye-tracking technology to peek into the real-time processing of language, focusing on how autistic adults read between the lines.

Rather than asking whether autistic adults read differently, we ask when and how differences emerge. Participants read short passages in which a simple phrase such as "I understand" can be a plain acknowledgement or a quiet sign of disappointment. By watching how the eyes move, pause and look back across the text, we can trace the time-course of inference processing. This shows the exact stage of comprehension where meaning breaks down, and how Theory of Mind, working memory, inhibitory control and central coherence shape it.

The goal is to turn these insights into evidence-based interventions, inclusive teaching and fairer assessment, so that every reader has equal access to learning.
